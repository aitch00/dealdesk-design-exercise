## Work context
- Prepares outgoing messages on several projects at the same time [BRIEF].
- Follows 4–6 active deals, with several different deal owners [ASSUMPTION].
- Is often interrupted and comes back to a message several times [ASSUMPTION].

## Goals
- Send the right message to the right people, without slowing down.
- Know at once if they can send and, if not, what to do.
- Not chase the deal owner for confirmations they could settle themselves.

## Frustrations
- Generic warnings that do not say what to fix [BRIEF].
- Not knowing if a request is approved or still waiting.
- Running a check again without knowing what changed.

## Behaviour
- Works fast [BRIEF].
- Recognises familiar names and companies at a glance [BRIEF].
- Uses the keyboard and shortcuts a lot [ASSUMPTION].
- Tends to close warnings that look repetitive [ASSUMPTION].

## Typical risks and errors
- **Association by familiarity** [BRIEF]: "Sam works for Corvane, Corvane is the Harbor client, so Sam is on the deal."
- Treating "I asked for approval" as "it is approved".
- Editing a reviewed draft and thinking the check still counts.
- Mixing up the active project when several drafts are open.

## What Jordan needs from the interface
- The active project always visible and impossible to confuse.
- For each recipient: on the deal team or not, with the role.
- The overall status in one sentence.
- The explanation next to the content it is about.
- Direct actions (fix) kept apart from actions that need someone else (ask for an exception).
- The reason a send is blocked, written next to the button.

## Accessibility and devices
- Desktop as main use; phone to check status on the move [ASSUMPTION].
- Full keyboard navigation, with visible focus.
- Status must be clear without telling colours apart.
- Works with a screen reader: labels, and announcements when the status changes.

## Representative quote
> "I don't need you to stop me on everything. I need you to stop me on what matters, and tell me why." *(invented)*
