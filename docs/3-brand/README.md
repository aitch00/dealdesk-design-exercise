# Brand

DealDesk is a fictional product, and the brief gave no brand. It allowed "a simple fictional brand identity" (WEB-18) and said no client design system is supplied (WEB-18b). So I made one.

**All brand content is based on assumptions.** The brief gives the name, the category, the customers and the limits; these are marked [BRIEF]. Everything else (history, city, size, voice, colours, type) is mine and is marked [ASSUMPTION] where it matters. Nothing here comes from research with users or buyers.

## A living reference

When a client has no brand, the brand becomes a shared reference. It is not finished at the start. It grew with the project:

- the personality and the voice came first, from the brief and the personas;
- the messaging was rewritten when the homepage changed;
- the visual identity was chosen from three directions on 10 November, then changed four more times as the app was checked: colour for states (CL1), the simulation on a yellow note (CL2), sheets on a desk (DP1–DP6), the type scale (UG1), and a wordmark without the caret (CL9).

Each change is a decision in the [decision register](../decisions.md), with what it replaced and why. The values (colours, sizes, shadows) live in one file only, `prototype/css/tokens.css`. These pages explain the choices; they do not repeat every value.

## Common rules

- **Everything is fictional.** No real company, person or figure.
- **Labels:** [BRIEF] comes from the brief · [ASSUMPTION] is invented by me · [TO DECIDE] is an open decision.
- The personas are **proto-personas** from the brief, not research (decision X1).
- Product copy is in English, the language of the prototype (decision X5).
